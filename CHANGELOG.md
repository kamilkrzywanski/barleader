# Changelog

The release workflow takes the section of the tagged version as the notes of its GitHub release.

## Unreleased

### Reading

- After a miss, or with `findAll`, fast mode reads each crop of `BarSegments` again in five ways, none of which takes an attempt: padded with 8 white pixels, as a tight crop cuts the bars at its ends where the hybrid binarizer finds no quiet zone; turned by 90 degrees, as lines of text beside a symbol can join its cluster and turn the crop along the text; sampled again from the frame 1.8 times as long, as the bars of a cluster can stop short of the guards; located again inside a crop of 360 pixels or more, whose small symbol has few edges on the 768-pixel raster of the frame; and for EAN-8 and UPC-E thresholded by Otsu and by the local mean, as the longer codes already were. A short code counts when the two thresholds, or both halves of its crop, read the same text, and a UPC-E only when the frame shows no bar just past its ends: a crop that ends at the middle guard of an EAN-13 reads its left half as a UPC-E. On the 2,868 retail codes of the public tapirscan benchmark, read with `findAll`, fast mode reads 2,703 instead of 2,646, 513 of the 558 turned photos instead of 477, and still no wrong text. On the snapshot it reads 3 more Arte-Lab photos (378 to 381) and 4 more KAIST photos, and with `findAll` 3 more Arte-Lab photos, 4 more KAIST photos and 8 more BarBeR codes; no wrong text is new, and on KAIST's photos without a barcode it takes about 4 ms more. On one BarBeR parcel a short Code 128 beside the labelled one, which the set leaves unlabelled, now comes back in its place.
- With `findAll` fast mode takes 16 crops of `BarSegments` instead of 8, and reads the ninth to the sixteenth after the thresholds of the first eight: read before them, they spent the attempts those thresholds needed, and a KAIST photo was lost.
- Aggressive mode takes every step of fast mode before its own: the variants turned by 90 degrees, the patches of `BarStrips`, the crops of `BarSegments` and their rescue, the deblurred crops, the fits, the EAN halves and the colour channels, with the frame halved from a shorter side of 800 as in fast mode. It used to share only the variants and then go its own way, and read fewer photos than fast mode: on KAIST 1,738 against 1,791, on the tapirscan benchmark it missed 48 turned and 28 vertical photos fast mode read. The aggressive steps after fast mode's get the attempts those took on top of their 100. Aggressive mode now reads at least as many as fast mode on every set: 66 more KAIST photos (1,738 to 1,804, 35 wrong instead of 37), 10 more Arte-Lab photos (389 to 399), one more KAIST EAN-8, 4 more zxing blackbox images and 53 more BarBeR codes (3,941 to 3,994), and with `findAll` 2,767 codes of the tapirscan benchmark instead of 2,651, every one fast mode reads among them. A photo fast mode reads wrong it reads wrong too: one Muenster EAN-13 it used to read right. Two BarBeR parcels return a second Code 128 the set leaves unlabelled in place of the labelled one. On the Arte-Lab photos it takes 0.15 s a photo instead of 0.21 s, on KAIST's photos without a barcode 0.51 s instead of 0.46 s.
- With `findAll`, a read of aggressive mode's band crops that lies on the scan line of a code already read with other content is left out, as with the crops of fast mode. On the tapirscan benchmark aggressive mode read 10 wrong texts, most of them an EAN-8 or UPC-E across the bars of the one it had read right, one or two digits off; it now reads 6, and one right read is hidden behind a wrong one read first.

### Speed

- The Gaussian threshold of aggressive mode applies its kernel, as wide as a twelfth of the shorter side, as about 22 nested boxes: each pass adds up a prefix sum along the row, or a running sum down the column, per box, so the cost no longer grows with the window, and every pixel is the same as tap by tap. On a 12-megapixel frame the threshold takes 0.5 s instead of 10.4 s, on a 2-megapixel one 75 ms instead of 0.76 s. Past 16 megapixels three box means of equal width stand for the kernel, where it took minutes that no budget stopped.
- Aggressive mode checks its time budget before it builds the Sauvola thresholds, one window at a time, the 1.5 and 0.5 scales and the inverted finders, and its region localizer builds only the narrow window. With `findAll`, the retail symbologies and the default budget of 8 s, aggressive mode returned from a dense 48-megapixel photo after 91 s and from a 100-megapixel one after 315 s; it now returns after 6.8 s and 8.8 s with the same code. On the nine upsampled photos of 12 to 48 megapixels of the tapirscan benchmark it took 49 s on average and up to 106 s, now 5.1 s and at most 7.4 s, and on its 20 large canvases 5.8 s and up to 23 s, now 2.6 s and at most 4.3 s, reading the same codes.
- The variants of the whole frame read linear symbols only on the rows that cross a box of `BarSegments` or `BarStrips`, widened by a tenth of its longer side, or that run along an edge of the frame, within a twentieth of its shorter side and at least 32 pixels: on 794 snapshot photos a variant read, the read lay in such a box on every one, and on photos without a barcode the boxes cover about an eighth of the frame. A variant reads QR Code and Micro QR only when the frame shows a finder ring, Aztec only when it shows a bullseye, and PDF417 only when it shows a start or stop pattern and the locators found something. The second read of the original image, with the global binarizer, reads only the symbologies of the black matrix: the linear readers threshold each row on its own, so on the same pixels they read again what the first read did. The two locators and the signs of the matrix symbologies are found side by side, the traces of QR Code, Micro QR, Aztec and Data Matrix share one matrix of the frame, and those of the inverted frame late in aggressive mode one of their own, the two fits share the profiles of a strip, a variant turned by 90 degrees turns only the rows it reads, and the black point of a row is estimated once. None of this changes a read: on all 16,382 snapshot images fast and aggressive mode, and fast mode with `findAll`, return the same texts as before, and so does aggressive mode with `findAll` on the 2,868 codes of the tapirscan benchmark. Read in turn with the previous version by the default reader on 9 threads, fast mode takes 15% less CPU on the Arte-Lab photos and 25% less on 400 of KAIST's photos without a barcode, and returns from those a fifth sooner; aggressive mode takes 10% and 6% less.
- Without `findAll`, after the two reads of the original image, the patches of `BarStrips` and the crops of `BarSegments` with their rescue are read first, as tapirscan reads its candidates before anything else, and the variants of the whole frame are built only when those read nothing. A crop is read on its own pixels, and read before the variants crops returned a wrong EAN-13 or UPC-A on 6 snapshot photos a variant read right, 5 of them with a wrong left half, and a UPC-E cut from an EAN-13 on 2. So an EAN-13 or UPC-A that none of the eleven lines of `EanCrossCheck` beside it repeats, and every UPC-E, wait for the variants and the finders, and count only when those read nothing. With the change above, fast mode takes 29% less CPU on the Arte-Lab photos than before and returns 10% sooner, aggressive mode 20% less CPU and 9% sooner; on photos without a barcode this change adds nothing to the one above. No labelled read is lost and no wrong text is new. Fast mode reads 2 more KAIST photos (1,795 to 1,797, wrong texts 33 to 31) and 5 more BarBeR codes (3,959 to 3,964), and aggressive mode 2 more KAIST photos (1,804 to 1,806) and 5 more BarBeR codes (3,994 to 3,999): on two packages the EAN-13 now comes back before the QR Code beside it, and on parcels the labelled Code 128 before a shorter code beside it. One BoofCV photo returns the UPC-A beside its QR Code instead of the QR Code. With `findAll` nothing changes.
- The patches of `BarStrips`, the crops of `BarSegments`, their thresholds and their rescue are read side by side instead of one after another, without `findAll` first and with it after the finders. Their reads are taken in order on the calling thread, each under the conditions it had one after another, so a read counts only where it counted before, and what can no longer count is not read: the thresholds of the crops, for one, once anything is read. On the 1,501 images of the miss corpus every text and every count of attempts is the same in fast and aggressive mode, with and without `findAll`. Read in turn with the previous version by the default reader on 9 threads, fast mode returns from the Arte-Lab photos 8% sooner and aggressive mode 7%, fast mode from KAIST's photos without a barcode 5% sooner, and fast mode with `findAll` from the Arte-Lab photos 10%.
- A variant of the whole frame decodes PDF417 only when a start or stop pattern of the frame lies in a region of the linear locators, not anywhere in it: each of the 73 PDF417 that variants read on the snapshot photos has one there, and of 300 KAIST photos without a barcode 94 pass instead of 144. On the 232 snapshot images with a PDF417 and 881 images without a barcode, fast and aggressive mode and fast mode with `findAll` return the same texts, and fast mode takes 4% less CPU on those without a barcode.
- After candidates and variants that read nothing, without `findAll`, the four finders, the deblurred crops, the Code 128 and EAN fits, the EAN halves and the colour channels run side by side, and their reads are taken in this order: each counts only while nothing before it was read, as before. With `findAll` the items of each of these steps run side by side. Every text and every count of attempts on the miss corpus is the same. Read in turn as above, against the two changes before, fast mode returns from KAIST's photos without a barcode 31% sooner and from the Arte-Lab photos 13%, aggressive mode 8% and 12%, and fast mode from the zxing blackbox images 24%.
- With the three changes above, fast mode returns from the Arte-Lab photos 20% sooner than before them and aggressive mode 17%, fast mode from KAIST's photos without a barcode 35% and aggressive mode 8% sooner, fast mode with `findAll` from the Arte-Lab photos 21%, and fast mode from the zxing blackbox images 31%. Where nothing is read they take the CPU time they took before: 2.5% more on KAIST's photos without a barcode, read on eight equal cores. Where a step after a miss reads, the steps after it have already started and their work is thrown away, about 40 ms a photo: fast mode takes 10–14% more CPU time on the Arte-Lab photos. With `findAll` no work is thrown away, and fast mode still takes 17% more on them, the price of running more of a read at once on the power-limited laptop processor they were measured on.
- The runs of a row are measured in one pass over its luminances, without a row of booleans, and its quarter-pixel edges, which Code 128, Code 39 and EAN/UPC read when no whole-pixel row reads, compare the sharpened row with the black point in whole numbers: only an edge needs the position between two pixels. The runs are the same on all 2.5 million rows of 350 photos compared, and so is every text and count of attempts on the miss corpus. Read one after another on one core with the retail symbologies, fast mode takes 9% less time on KAIST's photos without a barcode and as much as before on the Arte-Lab photos; with the default reader the difference stays within 2%.
- The QR finder patterns and the Aztec centres of the trace matrix of a frame are found once: the signs that let the variants read QR Code, Micro QR and Aztec, and the QR, Micro QR and Aztec traces, each looked for them on that same matrix again. Every text and count of attempts on the miss corpus is the same, and so are the texts of the zxing blackbox and BoofCV QR images. Read one after another on one core, fast mode takes 5% less time on KAIST's photos without a barcode; with the default reader the difference stays within 1%.

## 0.2.0

### Reading

- Fast mode halves a frame from a shorter side of 800 pixels instead of 1000, so the 1152×864 Arte-Lab photos get the halved attempt too: 321 labels read where 297 were, with the same 5 wrong texts. Aggressive mode keeps 1000.
- After a miss, fast mode locates linear symbols with `BarSegments`, a port of the segment voting of tapirscan 1.5.0, instead of the coarse linear-band search. With `findAll` a band read that lies on the scan line of another code already read is dropped.
- On a halved or quartered frame an EAN/UPC text is dropped when another text reads on more rows of the same bitmap, at whole or at quarter pixels. With `findAll` this removes 4 wrong texts on BarBeR, 2 on Arte-Lab and 1 on KAIST's multi-code photos, and no labelled read; aggressive mode is unchanged on all 16,382 snapshot images.
- When nothing was read, both modes sharpen the blurred crops of `BarSegments` three ways, after the deblurring of tapirscan 1.5.0: a motion ghost removed behind or ahead, and the defocus deconvolved, then read EAN-13 and UPC-A there. A text counts only when no copy reads another and the copies, their rows or `EanFit` confirm it. Fast mode reads 300 more images of the snapshot, 38 of them Arte-Lab photos (321 to 359), aggressive mode 53 more; nothing read before is lost and the negative sets stay clean. One of the new Arte-Lab reads, `Foto(839)`, counts as wrong: the label says 5322825005431, the digits printed under the bars are 7321925005431, which is what is read.
- A `BarSegments` box longer than 2,400 or taller than 1,200 pixels, a symbol close to the lens, is sampled more coarsely instead of dropped: fast mode reads 32 more EAN-13 of the snapshot, among them three sharp Arte-Lab photos it missed.
- In fast mode the contrast stretch, the illumination, the mean and Otsu thresholds and the halved frame are also read turned by 90 degrees, for the linear formats, after all other variants and without an attempt slot. The variants scanned rows only, so a vertical symbol that needed one of them was lost: of 930 Arte-Lab and KAIST photos turned by 90 degrees fast mode read 805, now 827. On the snapshot fast mode reads 57 more images, among them three more KAIST EAN-8, and nothing it read before; on photos without a barcode it takes about 20 ms more.
- When fast mode read nothing, not even with the fits, it joins EAN-13 and UPC-A from halves read on different rows of the image, its halved frame and the image turned by 90 degrees, after tapirscan's partial halves: glare or a fold that spoils every row in one place rarely spoils the same half. A text needs each half on four rows, and halves of five equal digits, which the bars of Royal Mail and of QR codes give, are refused. Fast mode reads 63 more images of the snapshot, 17 of them Arte-Lab photos (361 to 378), and no wrong text; on photos without a barcode it takes about 7 ms more.
- Every EAN-13 and UPC-A read is checked against the unmodified image, after tapirscan's source contradiction: eleven lines parallel to its scan line, shifted across the bars, are read again. When none of them repeats the read and two or more agree on another EAN-13, that text is returned instead; when they read several others, the read is dropped. On the snapshot this turned 14 wrong texts into their labels in fast mode and 14 in aggressive mode, mostly a wrong left half beside the right half of the real code, and with findAll it merged 47 such wrong second reads into the code beside them.
- A frame of 20 megapixels or more is read where it holds anything: blocks of 32 pixels whose luminance hardly changes are blank, and the box around the rest is read when it leaves out a quarter of the frame. A photo pasted on a blank 100-megapixel canvas was read whole, every variant thresholded the blank too, and fast mode ran out of time before it got to the photo: on 20 such canvases, rebuilt from the public tapirscan benchmark's photos and parameters, fast mode now reads 20 instead of 6, in 0.34 s instead of 2.7 s, and aggressive mode 20 instead of 16, in 0.34 s instead of 4.9 s.
- In fast mode a frame still that large is first read on a box-averaged copy of about eight megapixels, and whole when that reads nothing; with findAll it is read whole. An EAN-8 upsampled to 48 megapixels read on every reduced copy and on none of the full frame, whose modules outgrew the threshold windows. On the nine upsampled photos of 12 to 48 megapixels of the same benchmark fast mode reads 9 instead of 8, in 0.31 s instead of 0.74 s.
- An upright QR Code that fills its image reads without its finder patterns when the finder search finds none: the box around its dark pixels, leaving out bands dark from edge to edge, is sampled as a grid of 21, 25, ... modules in each quarter turn until the decoder's format and error correction accept one. BoofCV's pathological samples cut the corners off the finders, put a dot in their light rings, pierce their centres or run a dark band through the quiet zone; fast mode reads all 23 of them instead of 11, aggressive mode 23 instead of 12, and nothing else of the snapshot changes. The speed does not measurably change.
- When fast mode read nothing at all on a colour image, it reads the brightest and the darkest channel of each pixel as its very last step. Luma turns a red background into a dark gray under black bars and blue bars into a middling gray on white; the brightest channel keeps the first contrast, the darkest the second. Fast mode reads 8 more images of the snapshot, an EAN-13 on red among them, and no wrong text; on colour photos without a barcode it takes about 11 ms more. Earlier in the ladder the channels read a short Code 128 on parcels before the band crops and fits reached the labelled one.

## 0.1.1

### Fixed

- With `findAll`, a linear symbol read on two rows far apart, or along a slant through the bars another attempt read straight, came back twice: a tall Code 93 read at its bottom row and at its top, 173 px apart on a 272 px symbol. Two scan lines of the same text are now one symbol when they cross or nearly touch, or when the bars run unbroken from one to the other. Two symbols of the same text side by side or stacked stay two. ## 0.1.0

The first release. Java 17 or later, no dependencies at run time; on the module path the module is `io.github.kamilkrzywanski.barleader`, and only its package of that name is exported.

### Reading

- QR Code, Micro QR, Data Matrix, Aztec, PDF417, Code 128, Code 39, Code 93, EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and UPC-E with their 2- and 5-digit add-ons, ITF, Codabar, and GS1 DataBar, DataBar Limited and DataBar Expanded, from photos and scans. MaxiCode, Telepen, DX film edge, rMQR, MicroPDF417, Royal Mail (RM4SCC) and Japan Post are experimental and read only when asked for.
- The detectors and bit decoders are a Java port of the read path of zxing-cpp 3.1.1; the binarizers come from ZXing 3.5.4, and every decoder corrects errors and erasures with the Reed-Solomon decoder of zxing-cpp's librscpp.
- `BarcodeReader` tries a ladder of image variants until one decodes: `ReadOptions.fast()` by default, `ReadOptions.aggressive()` for hard images, with `findAll`, a choice of symbologies, attempt and time budgets, and a parallel, a custom-executor or a sequential reader. `BarcodeHit` gives the text, the bytes, the corners, the orientation and the attempt that read it.
- On the zxing-cpp sample images fast mode reads 460 of 464 symbols, where Java ZXing with `TRY_HARDER` reads 337; on the Arte-Lab EAN-13 photos fast reads 291 and aggressive 389 of 430, ZXing 205; on BarBeR, without its Arte-Lab and KAIST parts, fast reads 3,857 and aggressive 3,920 of 5,176 codes, ZXing 2,484. The README has the full tables and how to repeat them.

### Writing

- `BarcodeWriter` writes every symbology of the ZXing writers and of Barcode-Lib4J 3.2.0: QR Code and GS1 QR Code, Data Matrix and GS1 Data Matrix, PDF417, Aztec, Code 128 with its code sets, GS1-128, EAN-14, SSCC-18, Code 39 and its extended form, PZN and PZN8, Code 93 and its extended form, Codabar, Code 11, ITF, EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A, UPC-E with add-ons, ISBN-13 and ISMN. GS1 element strings are checked against their application identifiers.
- For the same choices every module is the one ZXing or Barcode-Lib4J writes. Two bugs of ZXing's minimal Data Matrix encoder are fixed: the 12×36 rectangle sized as 33 codewords, and data ending in EDIFACT without the unlatch.
- `QrCode` writes QR Code with every setting of its own: level, version, mask, character set, GS1 and compact segments.
- A `Barcode` draws itself as an image, an SVG, a one-page vector PDF without any library, or on any `Graphics2D`. Linear symbols get their human-readable line, EAN and UPC laid out as on a retail label; `RenderOptions` sets the bar height, the font, or no line.

### Command line

- `java -jar barleader.jar image...` prints one line or one JSON object per symbol; `write --type=...` and `qr` write a PNG, an SVG or a PDF.

### Known limitations

- MaxiCode is read only as a clean symbol that fills the image, without a detector for photos; MaxiCode, Telepen and DX film edge return the corners of the whole image. QR Model 1, Han Xin and DotCode are not read, and ECI, structured-append and symbology-identifier metadata are not in `BarcodeHit` yet.
- The human-readable line needs a font from AWT, which a headless JDK without fonts does not have; `RenderOptions.humanReadable(false)` draws without it.
- The API may still change before 1.0.
